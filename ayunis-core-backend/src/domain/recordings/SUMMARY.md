Recordings
Meeting recordings owned by a user, a workspace, a team or the organization

A recording has a name, an organization (`orgId`), exactly one owner, the user who created it (`createdByUserId`, null once that user is deleted), timestamps and at most one uploaded audio file with its transcript. The summary follows in a later step of AYC-1648. The module is behind `FEATURE_RECORDINGS_ENABLED` (`recordingsEnabled`, off by default): with it off, every `/recordings` route returns 404, while the permissions, the data and the deletion listeners stay.

**Ownership.** The domain subclasses `PersonalRecording` (`userId`), `WorkspaceRecording` (`workspaceId`), `TeamRecording` (`teamId`) and `OrgRecording` form the `Recording` union; `CollectiveRecording` is team or org, `TransferableRecording` every owner but a workspace. Persistence uses the shared owner shape from `src/domain/ownership/`: `@SingleOwnerCheck('recordings')` (one of `userId`, `workspaceId`, `teamId` or `isOrgOwned`) and `whereCollectiveOwner` for team and org rows. `application/models/recording-owner.ts` aliases the shared `ResourceOwner` types and builds the recording for an owner (`recordingOwnedBy`), which create and owner change share.

**Access.** `RecordingAccessService` decides from the persisted owner, after checking the organization:

- Personal: only the owner reads and manages.
- Team and org: `CollectiveRecordingAccessService` (a `CollectiveResourceAccessService` with `manage_recordings`, `manage_team_recordings`, `manage_org_recordings`) applies the teams module's `GetCollectiveOwnerAccessUseCase`: members of the team or organization read; managing also takes `manage_recordings` plus the team permission as resolved for the user's team role, or the org permission.
- Workspace: the exported workspace read and write access use cases.

Unreadable recordings are `RECORDING_NOT_FOUND` (404); readable but unmanageable team and org recordings are forbidden (403). `requireCreate` is for recordings about to exist for, or move to, an owner: an unmanageable team or organization is forbidden rather than hidden. Every managing route also requires `manage_recordings` (`@RequirePermission`).

**Audio and transcript.** The audio is a source of the sources module: a `FileSource` that keeps the retained original (`storageKey`), the formatted transcript text, its search index and the transcript segments, and points at its recording through `sources.recordingId` (unique, `ON DELETE CASCADE`). `CreateRecordingUploadUseCase` reserves a direct upload (target type `recording`) after `requireWrite`, only while the recording has no audio (`RECORDING_AUDIO_ALREADY_UPLOADED`, 409); the sources module accepts only audio (MP3, M4A, WAV, WebM) up to `AUDIO_MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES` and a permitted embedding model, so a doomed file is refused before bytes move. An upload that passes every check is charged to the uploader's recording fair-use quota (`QuotaType.FAIR_USE_RECORDINGS` through the quotas module's `CheckQuotaUseCase`, passed as the session's `admit` callback; default 5 per hour, set by super admins, skipped for usage-based subscriptions) before the session is saved; over the limit the upload is refused with `QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (429). Abandoned or failed uploads keep their charge. `CompleteRecordingUploadUseCase` runs `CompleteSourceUploadUseCase` and, in its transaction, locks the recording and attaches the source (`RecordingRepository.attachAudio`, guarded by `NOT EXISTS` and the unique index). The upload queueing marks recording jobs `keepTranscriptSegments`, so document processing transcribes them in one request with diarization (`TranscribeRecordingUseCase`), stores the timed segments with the provider's speaker label apart from the text and indexes whole speaker turns as chunks whose meta carries the time range (see the sources and transcriptions summaries). The recording's status comes from its audio source (`recordingStatusOf`): no audio `uploading`, `PROCESSING` `transcribing`, `READY` `transcribed`, `FAILED` `failed` with the source's `processingErrorCode` as `failureCode` (`RECORDING_TOO_LONG` above `AUDIO_MAX_DURATION_SECONDS` or when the provider refuses it). Transient failures are retried by the document queue; a terminal one stays until the user deletes the recording and uploads again. `GetRecordingTranscriptUseCase` returns the segments as speaker turns (`toTranscriptTurns`: consecutive segments of one speaker merged, speakers numbered by first appearance) after `requireRead`. `RecordingDetailsService` adds the creator's name and the audio to list and detail results, one lookup each.

**Use cases.** `CreateRecordingUseCase` (any owner), `ListRecordingsUseCase` (personal scope: the user's own plus readable team and org recordings in one query; workspace scope: one workspace after read access), `FindRecordingUseCase`, `RenameRecordingUseCase`, `ChangeRecordingOwnerUseCase` and `DeleteRecordingUseCase`. List and find return the creator's name (`RecordingCreatorNameService`, through the users module). Rename, owner change and delete lock the row. An owner change moves between the caller, a team and the organization, needs the right to manage both owners, keeps id, creator and creation date, and rejects workspace recordings with `RECORDING_OWNER_CHANGE_NOT_ALLOWED` (only after access is checked).

**Deletion.** Rows go with their owner by FK cascade: deleting a user deletes their personal recordings and, with the workspaces they own, those workspaces' recordings; team and org recordings they created stay with `createdByUserId` set to null. Deleting a team, workspace or organization deletes its recordings. `RecordingCleanupService` is where every deletion path ends: `DeleteRecordingUseCase` calls it after its commit, and the user, team, workspace and organization deletion listeners snapshot the affected recordings before the cascade and defer it until the owner's row is gone. The FK cascade from the recording removes its audio source with the transcript text, segments and index entries; `RecordingCleanupService` snapshots the audio (`snapshot`, before the delete) and afterwards runs `CleanupSourceProcessingUseCase` per organization, which cancels a queued or running transcription, purges its processing results and removes the uploaded original once nothing references it. Listener failures are logged and never block the deletion.

**HTTP.** `RecordingsController` at `/recordings`: `POST /` (`ownerType` `personal`, `workspace` with `workspaceId`, `team` with `teamId`, or `org`), `GET /?ownerType=personal|workspace[&workspaceId=]`, `GET /:id`, `GET /:id/transcript` (speaker turns), `PATCH /:id` (rename), `PATCH /:id/owner` (`personal`, `team` with `teamId`, or `org`) and `DELETE /:id`. `RecordingUploadsController`: `POST /:id/uploads` and `POST /:id/uploads/:uploadId/complete` (both `manage_recordings`); the bytes go through the generic `/source-uploads/:id/sign` endpoints. Responses carry `ownerType`, `workspaceId` or `teamId`, `creatorName`, `status`, `failureCode` (failed only) and timestamps. Every route is behind `@RequireFeature(FeatureFlag.Recordings)`.

**Wiring.** `RecordingsModule` imports `LocalRecordingRepositoryModule` (which also registers `SourceRecord` for the audio link), `SourcesModule`, `QuotasModule`, the transcription config, `ContextModule`, `TeamsApplicationModule`, `UsersModule` and `WorkspacesModule`. Transcription runs where the document consumers run (`IngestionConsumersModule`: the API, or the separate ingestion worker). `local-recording.repository.integration.spec.ts` runs the real migrations and proves the access query, the single-owner check, the deletion cascades (including the audio and its segments), one audio per recording and the permission backfill.
